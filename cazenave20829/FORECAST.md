# Pre-computation forecast — 2026-11-10

Selected problem: Tristan Cazenave, *Lemmas and Conjectures in Spectral Graph Theory*, arXiv:2611.03879v1, §7 Conjecture 2 (#20829). For every connected finite simple graph with at least two vertices, is the second-largest adjacency eigenvalue at most the largest Laplacian eigenvalue minus 2? Working difficulty estimate: 24/100, with RH at 100. This is a subjective selection score.

Forecast: a finite disproof is the likeliest outcome. Joining two dense blocks with one degree-2 connector, at a vertex whose degree was lowered by deleting an internal edge, might preserve a high second adjacency eigenvalue while raising the largest Laplacian eigenvalue less than a direct bridge or a bridge between complete cliques. I will first compare equal near-cliques with complete-clique controls and vary the missing edge and bridge placement. A spectral quotient, Rayleigh witness, or rational positive-definiteness certificate should settle the signs if a violation is found. If that family fails, independent structural and spectral searches will test the universal inequality.
