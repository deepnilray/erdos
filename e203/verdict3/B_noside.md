The best unconditional statement toward "every m has a prime" is still Erdős–Odlyzko 1979, Theorem 2 with p_1=2, p_2=3: the m coprime to 6 with a prime 2^a3^b m+1 have positive lower density (constant effective, never made explicit). ## 1. Notation

For a prime p≥5 put H_p=⟨2,3⟩⊂F_p^×, e_p=|H_p|, Λ_p={(i,j)∈ℤ²: 2^i3^j≡1 mod p}. For squarefree d coprime to 6 put Λ_d=∩_{p|d}Λ_p, e(d)=[ℤ²:Λ_d]=|⟨2,3⟩ mod d|. N_{k,ℓ}(m)=2^k3^ℓ m+1. Throughout gcd(m,6)=1.

**Lemma B (killed set) [PROVED].** For a prime p≥5:
(i) |⟨2,3⟩| = lcm(ord_p 2, ord_p 3), because F_p^× is cyclic. Hence e(d) is a multiple of ord_d(2) for every d, and e(d)≥ord_d(2).
(ii) The map (i,j)↦2^i3^j mod p is a surjective homomorphism ℤ²→H_p with kernel Λ_p, so [ℤ²:Λ_p]=e_p.
(iii) {(k,ℓ)∈ℤ²: p | N_{k,ℓ}(m)} is empty if p|m or −m^{-1}∉H_p, and otherwise a single coset of Λ_p.
(iv) For the lattice Λ=Λ_d let h=ord_d(3) and let cℤ be the projection of Λ to the first coordinate (c is the order of 2 in (ℤ/d)^×/⟨3⟩). Then Λ∩({0}×ℤ)={0}×hℤ and e(d)=c·h.
*Proof.* (i) In a cyclic group the subgroup generated by x,y has order lcm(ord x, ord y). (ii) is the first isomorphism theorem. (iii) N≡0 mod p iff 2^k3^ℓ≡−m^{-1}; this set is empty or a coset of the kernel. (iv) (0,j)∈Λ iff 3^j≡1. The projection of Λ is {i: 2^i∈⟨3⟩ mod d}=cℤ. ℤ²/Λ maps onto ℤ/cℤ with kernel (cℤ×ℤ)/Λ≅ℤ/hℤ, so the index is c·h. ∎

**Lemma A (no exceptional zeros for 3-smooth moduli) [PROVED].** A primitive real Dirichlet character whose conductor is of the form 2^a3^b has conductor in {1,3,4,8,12,24}.
*Proof.* Primitive real characters correspond to fundamental discriminants D with conductor |D|. A fundamental discriminant is either D≡1 mod 4 and squarefree, or D=4d with d≡2,3 mod 4 squarefree. |D|=2^a3^b forces the squarefree part into {±1,±2,±3,±6}. D≡1 mod 4 and odd gives D∈{1,−3}, conductors 1, 3. D=4d with d∈{−1,2,−2,3,6,−6} (d=1 and d=−3 are ≡1 mod 4, d=−1 is ≡3) gives D∈{−4,8,−8,12,24,−24}, conductors 4,8,8,12,24,24. ∎
Control [VERIFIED]: `B_conductors.py` enumerates all |D|=2^a3^b with a<30, b<20 and tests fundamentality independently with sympy; output [1,3,4,8,12,24].
*Consequence (cited, not re-proved).* This is the reason EO79's Lemma 1 can be run with no Siegel-zero loss: there are only finitely many, explicitly known, real characters to rule out (the seven L-functions of conductors 3,4,8,12,24). I did not check the zero-free statement for those L-functions myself.

## 2. The theorem and its proof architecture

**Theorem (Erdős–Odlyzko 1979, Thm 2, r=2, p=(2,3)) [PROVED in the literature; I read the statement and a summary of the proof on the primary scan].** There are effectively computable constants c>0, x_0 such that for x>x_0 at least c·x integers m<x with gcd(m,6)=1 have 2^a3^b m+1 prime for some a,b (the exponents are taken ≥0 or ≥1 according to EO's convention, which the scan does not make legible).

*Skeleton (my reconstruction, consistent with the summary I read).* Fix N=⌊c_0 log x⌋ with c_0 small, B⊂[1,N]² a box of exponent vectors u=(a,b), A_u=2^a3^b, and let R(m)=#{u∈B: A_u m+1 prime} for m∈(x/2,x], gcd(m,6)=1.
- (I1) First moment. Σ_m R(m) ≫ x·|B|/log x. Needs primes p≡1 mod A_u with p≤A_u x, with (p−1)/A_u coprime to 6 (inclusion–exclusion over the moduli A_u, 2A_u, 3A_u, 6A_u; the main terms give the factor 1/3, which is the density of m coprime to 6). This needs primes in progressions to moduli up to 6·6^N≈x^{c_0 ln 6}, which is where Linnik-type zero-density estimates and Lemma A enter. **Cited: EO79 Lemma 1. Not re-proved.**
- (I2) Pair sieve. For u≠u': #{m∈(x/2,x], (m,6)=1: A_u m+1, A_{u'} m+1 both prime} ≤ C·x·F(u,u')/(log x)², uniformly in u,u', where F(u,u')=∏_{p≥5, p|A_{u'}−A_u}(1+1/(p−2)). This is the dimension-2 upper-bound sieve for two linear forms (Halberstam–Richert, Sieve Methods, Thm 2.3 as I remember it; EO79 cite Halberstam–Richert and Prachar). **Cited. I did not re-read the theorem, so treat the uniformity in A_u as an assumption. The loss in the error term is O(log log |E|/log x) with |E|≤x^{O(1)}, which is harmless.**
- (I3) Arithmetic of the pair factor: Σ_{u≠u'∈B}F(u,u') ≤ C_0|B|². **Proved below (Lemma D).**
- Conclusion. By (I1), ΣR ≫ x|B|/log x ≍ x log x. By (I2) and (I3), Σ_m R(m)(R(m)−1) ≪ (x/log²x)·Σ_{u≠u'}F(u,u') ≪ (x/log²x)·|B|² ≍ x log²x, so ΣR² ≪ x log x + x log²x. Cauchy–Schwarz gives #{m: R(m)>0} ≥ (ΣR)²/ΣR² ≫ x²log²x/(x log²x) = x. The two powers of log x match exactly, which is why the box must have |B|≍log²x (N≍log x); a smaller box makes the first moment too small for the second moment to be controlled.

**Lemma R (Romanoff for the pair) [PROVED].** R_0 := Σ_{d squarefree, (d,6)=1} 3^{ω(d)}/(d·ord_d(2)) < ∞.
*Proof.* Let P_y={p≥5: ord_p(2)≤y}. Each p∈P_y divides ∏_{t≤y}(2^t−1)<2^{y(y+1)/2}, so ∏_{p∈P_y}p<2^{y²}. If P_y has k elements, their product is at least the product of the first k primes ≥5, so Chebyshev's θ(x)≫x gives that the k-th smallest element q_k satisfies q_k≪y². By Mertens, Σ_{p∈P_y}1/p ≤ Σ_{p≤q_k}1/p ≤ log log y+O(1). Let T(y)=Σ_{d: ord_d(2)≤y}3^{ω(d)}/d. If ord_d(2)≤y then every prime p|d has ord_p(2)|ord_d(2), so p∈P_y; hence T(y)≤∏_{p∈P_y}(1+3/p)≤exp(3Σ_{P_y}1/p)≪(log y)³. Summation by parts: Σ_d 3^{ω(d)}/(d ord_d 2)=Σ_{t≥1}(T(t)−T(t−1))/t=Σ_t T(t)/(t(t+1)) ≪ Σ_t (log t)³/t² <∞. ∎

**Lemma D (pair correlation) [PROVED].** For (i,j)∈ℤ²\{0} let n(i,j) be the numerator of 2^i3^j−1 and F(i,j)=∏_{p≥5, p|n(i,j)}(1+1/(p−2)). There is an absolute C such that for N≥2: Σ_{0<max(|i|,|j|)≤N}F(i,j) ≤ C·N². Consequently, for B=[1,N]², Σ_{u≠u'∈B}F(u,u') ≤ C·N⁴ = C|B|².
*Proof.* p|A_{u'}−A_u with p≥5 iff 2^{i}3^{j}≡1 mod p, where (i,j)=u'−u, i.e. iff (i,j)∈Λ_p; so F(u,u')=F(i,j). Expanding the product, F(i,j)=Σ_{d|n(i,j), d squarefree, (d,6)=1}g(d) with g(d)=∏_{p|d}1/(p−2), and d|n(i,j) iff (i,j)∈Λ_d. So Σ F=Σ_d g(d)·#((Λ_d∩[−N,N]²)\{0}). By Lemma B(iv), the number of points of Λ_d in the box is at most (2N/c+1)(2N/h+1)=4N²/e(d)+2N/c+2N/h+1≤4N²/e(d)+4N+1 (for fixed i the admissible j form a coset of hℤ, and the admissible i form cℤ), so excluding the origin it is ≤4N²/e(d)+4N. Only d whose prime factors are ≤6^N occur, since n(i,j)≤6^N in absolute value. Therefore
ΣF ≤ 4N²·Σ_d g(d)/e(d) + 4N·∏_{5≤p≤6^N}(1+1/(p−2)).
Since 1/(p−2)≤3/p and e(d)≥ord_d(2) (Lemma B(i)), the first sum is at most R_0. By Mertens the product is ≪log(6^N)≪N. The pair statement follows because each difference vector arises from at most N² ordered pairs. ∎
Control [VERIFIED]: `B_lemmaR.py` computes the mean of F over the box |i|,|j|≤N with all primes 5≤p<10^5 (tail p>10^5 changes F by a factor <1+10^{-3}): mean F = 1.1655, 1.1685, 1.1683, 1.1680 for N=10, 20, 40, 80. It is flat in N, as the lemma requires (a wrong lemma, e.g. one with Σ1/(p e_p) replaced by Σ1/p, would grow like log N).

**Remark (k,ℓ≥1).** If EO79's Lemma 1 holds for a box [N/2,N]² of exponent vectors (it is stated for exponent vectors up to ≈c log x and I could not verify from the garbled scan whether they include 0), the same proof gives the theorem with a,b≥1, hence the form relevant to this repo. Lemma D is insensitive to the choice of box. I therefore label the k,ℓ≥1 version **PROVED modulo this reading of EO79 Lemma 1**, not PROVED outright.

## 3. What the unconditional methods cannot do

**3.1 The sieve cannot reach density one [PROVED as a statement about the method; the numbers are CONJECTURED/MODEL].**
Cauchy–Schwarz over m gives #{R>0} ≥ (ΣR)²/ΣR². Even if the pair-correlation sieve were sharp, ΣR²/(ΣR)² ≥ E[C²]/E[C]² where C(m) is the local factor (the density of cells not killed by small primes). `B_cvar.py` samples 2·10⁴ values of m∈[10⁹,10¹⁰] with C(m) computed from primes 5≤p≤3000 (independence Euler product, h_model.py convention): mean C=3.0105, E[C²]/E[C]²=1.0268, so the ideal Cauchy–Schwarz ratio is ≤0.974 and cannot be 1 (stratifying m by residue classes mod ∏_{p≤z}p removes this loss in the limit but not the next point). In addition, the upper-bound sieve for two forms loses a constant factor relative to the Hardy–Littlewood prediction (Selberg/Halberstam–Richert constant 8 against 2 for the twin-prime shape; I am quoting this from memory, **unverified**), which turns the ideal 0.97 into a number of order 1/4. Hence "positive lower density" is the natural ceiling of the EO method. Density one would need an asymptotic for pair correlations, which is the parity barrier.

**3.2 No P_r statement.** The number of cells with 2^k3^ℓ≤X is ≍ (log X)²; any sieve producing N_{k,ℓ} with Ω≤r needs a level of distribution about X^{1/r} for 2^k3^ℓ over residue classes of primes that large, while there are only (log X)² data points and the typical e_p is ≈p. I found no theorem of this type (APPROACHES.md C9). Nothing here is proved; it is a reason, not a theorem.

**3.3 What the verified covering bounds say per m (repo results, not re-proved).** DISTORTION.md proves that no covering (pure) with all e_p≤4·10⁵ exists, with maximal covered density B(4·10⁵)=0.730584; MIXED.md Theorem E extends this to mixed certificates. Reading this as a statement about every fixed m: **for every m coprime to 6, the set of (k,ℓ)∈ℤ² such that N_{k,ℓ}(m) is divisible by no prime of the pool {p: e_p≤4·10⁵} has positive lower density [PROVED in the repo].** **[CORRECTED 2026-10-26, V_check.md: the earlier claim "lower density ≥ 1−0.730584 = 0.2694" is WITHDRAWN. B(E) bounds the tilted measure P_final of the union, not uniform density; the 31 pool primes with e_p | 5040 cover uniform density 0.71705 > 1−B(2000)... see V_unif.py. Only positivity follows.]** Cells in this set have only large prime factors, but that is not primality, and the statement says nothing about the cells' prime factorisation beyond the pool.

## 4. Reformulation and necessary structure used above [PROVED]

For m with gcd(m,6)=1, "no prime among 2^k3^ℓ m+1, k,ℓ≥1" holds iff m is not of the form (p−1)/(2^k3^ℓ), k,ℓ≥1, for any prime p. The site's version (k,ℓ≥0) additionally excludes p=2^k m+1 (ℓ=0); the column k=0 is even and >2 whenever m>1 or ℓ>0. These are tautologies, listed because the code and the site use different conventions.

## 7. Far-tail test of the Cramér/Bateman–Horn model

**Setup** (`B_tailmodel.c`, `B_tail_compare.txt`, `B_tail_model_out.txt`). For 1.5·10^7 uniformly random m∈(3·10^9,10^10] coprime to 6, the cells (k≥1,ℓ≥1, 2^k3^ℓ<2^33) are sieved exactly by all primes 5≤p≤1500 (cell killed iff p | 2^k3^ℓ m+1), and each surviving cell gets probability ∏_{p≤1500}(1−1/p)^{−1}/ln(2^k3^ℓ m+1) of being prime, independently. This is the Cramér/Bateman–Horn model with exact small-prime lattice correlations. The model prediction for "first prime has s≥2^b" is the mean over sampled m of ∏_{cells s<2^b}(1−P_cell). ## 8. Aristotle prompts (Lean-formalisable statements)

**AP1 (conductor lemma, Lemma A).** In Lean 4 with Mathlib: define `IsFundDisc (D : ℤ) : Prop := (D % 4 = 1 ∧ Squarefree D) ∨ (∃ d : ℤ, D = 4 * d ∧ (d % 4 = 2 ∨ d % 4 = 3) ∧ Squarefree d)`. Prove: for all `a b : ℕ` and `D : ℤ` with `IsFundDisc D` and `D.natAbs = 2^a * 3^b`, we have `D.natAbs ∈ ({1, 3, 4, 8, 12, 24} : Finset ℕ)`. (Elementary: reduce to the squarefree part dividing 6.)

**AP2 (lattice point count, Lemma D core).** Let `c h N : ℕ` with `c ≥ 1`, `h ≥ 1`. Let `Λ : AddSubgroup (ℤ × ℤ)` satisfy: for all `j : ℤ`, `(0, j) ∈ Λ ↔ (h : ℤ) ∣ j`, and for all `i : ℤ`, `(∃ j, (i, j) ∈ Λ) ↔ (c : ℤ) ∣ i`. Prove: `((Finset.Icc (-(N:ℤ)) N ×ˢ Finset.Icc (-(N:ℤ)) N).filter (· ∈ Λ)).card ≤ (2*N/c + 1) * (2*N/h + 1)` where the right side is computed in `ℕ` with `/` being floor division (it is a valid upper bound; equivalently prove the real-number inequality `card ≤ (2*N/c+1)*(2*N/h+1)`). Proof idea: for fixed `i` the admissible `j` form a coset of `hℤ`, at most `⌊2N/h⌋+1` of them in `[-N,N]`; the admissible `i` form `cℤ`, at most `⌊2N/c⌋+1` of them.

**AP3 (killed set is a lattice coset, Lemma B(iii)).** Let `p ≥ 5` be prime, `m : ℤ` with `¬ (p : ℤ) ∣ m`, `e = Nat.lcm (orderOf (2 : ZMod p)) (orderOf (3 : ZMod p))`. Define `S = {(k,l) : ℕ × ℕ | (2^k * 3^l * (m : ZMod p) + 1 = 0)}`. Prove: (a) if `(k,l) ∈ S` then `(k+e, l) ∈ S` and `(k, l+e) ∈ S`; (b) if `(k,l) ∈ S` and `(k',l') ∈ S` then `2^k * 3^l = 2^k' * 3^l'` in `ZMod p`; (c) for `E` a multiple of `e` with `E > 0`, the number of `(k,l)` with `k,l < E` in `S` is either `0` or `E^2 / e`. (Part (c) uses that `(i,j) ↦ 2^i 3^j` is a surjection onto a subgroup of order `e` in the cyclic group `(ZMod p)ˣ`.)

**AP4 (reformulation, trivial).** For `m : ℕ` coprime to 6: `(∀ k l : ℕ, 1 ≤ k → 1 ≤ l → ¬ Nat.Prime (2^k*3^l*m+1)) ↔ ¬ ∃ p k l : ℕ, p.Prime ∧ 1 ≤ k ∧ 1 ≤ l ∧ p = 2^k*3^l*m+1`.

(Lemmas R and D need Romanoff-type bounds that Mathlib does not have; I do not offer them as Aristotle targets.)
