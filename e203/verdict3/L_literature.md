### L4. [LEAD] Z.-W. Sun / Lettl-Sun: size of minimal covers of abelian groups by cosets
Z.-W. Sun, "On covers of abelian groups by cosets", arXiv:math/0411144 (Acta Arith. 131 (2008) per impan.pl listing); Z.-W. Sun, "Finite covers of groups by cosets or subgroups", arXiv:math/0501451. Read both (2026-10-26).
Definitions: Mycielski function f(n) = sum over primes p|n of ord_p(n)(p-1); n <= 2^{f(n)}.
Thm 1.3 (math/0411144): Let {a_s G_s}_{s=1..k} be an m-cover of an abelian group G by cosets. For a in G with multiplicity w(a)=m put N_a = [G : intersection of G_s over s with a in a_s G_s]. Then N_a <= 2^{k-m} and k >= m + f(N_a). If {a_s G_s}_{s != t} is not an m-cover (coset t irredundant) then [G:G_t] <= 2^{k-m} and k >= m + f([G:G_t]). Bounds are best possible. (Paraphrase of the paper; the proof uses characters/algebraic number theory and the paper does not spell out the infinite-group case; for #203 apply it to the finite quotient Z^2/Lambda_S, which is legitimate since every certificate is periodic mod Lambda_S = intersection of the Lambda_p.)
Use for #203 [LEAD]: take a minimal sub-certificate with k primes p_1..p_k (all irredundant). With m=1, every p in it has k >= 1 + f(e_p) >= 1 + P^+(e_p) - 1, i.e. every prime in a minimal certificate has e_p that is (k-1)-smooth with sum ord_q(e_p)(q-1) <= k-1, so e_p <= 2^{k-1}. Moreover for a point a covered by a set T of primes only, k >= 1 + f(lcm_{p in T} e_p). So a certificate of size k lives inside the pool {p : f(e_p) <= k-1}. A prover must (i) count that pool as a function of k (primes dividing gcd(2^e-1,3^e-1) for e with f(e) <= k-1; e = 2-power times small primes) and show it cannot supply k primes with sum 1/e_p >= 1 and all pairwise overlaps; (ii) combine with the repo's result "no covering with all e_p <= 4e5": a certificate must contain an irredundant prime with e_p > 4e5 hence k >= 1+f(e_p) and e_p must be smooth; one can then enumerate e with e > 4e5 and f(e) <= K. This is a cheap, unconditional pruning not used in DISTORTION.md. Caveat: the pool for large K grows (e up to 2^{K-1}); the bound is weak when K is large. P.

### L5. [LEAD, partial] Multiplicity-version of the minimum modulus theorem (distortion method with repeated moduli)
arXiv:2212.01299 ("On the j-th smallest modulus of a covering system with distinct moduli"; authors not shown on the page I fetched; journal not shown), read 2026-10-26.
Thm 1: a minimal covering with distinct moduli has j-th smallest modulus <= exp(c j^2/log(j+1)). Thm 3 (as summarised by the fetch tool): a covering system of multiplicity s (each modulus used <= s times) has smallest modulus <= exp(c log^2(s+1)/log log(s+2)). Method: BBMST distortion + Crittenden-Vanden Eynden (n progressions covering first 2^n integers cover Z); duplicating moduli 2^{l-1} times.
Use for #203 [LEAD]: restrict a 2-D certificate to a lattice line {x0 + t v} (v=(a,b)): each prime p whose coset meets the line gives a residue class mod ord_p(2^a 3^b), so a 2-D certificate yields a 1-D covering of Z with moduli ord_p(2^a3^b) of multiplicity s_v = max_n #{p : ord_p(g_v)=n} <= max_n log(g_v-cyclotomic part)/log n (bounded by BCZ-type gcd bounds on p | 2^{na}3^{nb}-... i.e. primes with order dividing n). Thm 3 says: such a covering has min modulus <= exp(c log^2(s+1)/loglog(s+2)). If a prover finds a direction v for which every prime of the certificate has ord_p(g_v) greater than that bound, contradiction. Obstruction: small primes (5,7,13,...) have e_p tiny so ord_p(g_v) tiny in every direction; the argument only works once the repo's pool handling has removed or accounted for the small-e primes (e.g. apply to the residual set after the small primes, which is a union of cosets of a lattice; the restriction to a line of the residual is a 1-D 'incomplete' covering and the distortion machinery in its density form (Lemma 3.2 of 2211.01417) applies). P for the theorem statement as reported; the proof not checked.

### L6. Hough-Nielsen and BBMST: what they actually need
Hough-Nielsen, "Covering systems with restricted divisibility", arXiv:1703.02133 (Proc. AMS 2019): Thm 1: every covering with distinct moduli has a modulus divisible by 2 or 3; the proof shows: for distinct moduli > 1 coprime to 6, the uncovered set has positive density (Shearer-type bound rules out primes 5..631; staged induction with P_0=4,P_1=222,P_2=4000,P_{i+1}=P_i^{1.5}; Lovasz local lemma variant). Repeated moduli: not treated. P.
Balister-Bollobas-Morris-Sahasrabudhe-Tiba, "Erdős covering systems", arXiv:2211.01417 (survey/exposition, Acta Math. Hungar. per memory, journal not on the fetched page): Thm 2.1: if |S_k|>=2 and liminf |S_k|/k > 3 then hyperplanes covering S_1 x ... x S_n include two parallel ones or one with free-coordinate set F(A) in {1..C}. Lemma 3.2: if (1/(4 delta(1-delta))) sum_k E_{k-1}[alpha_k(x)^2] < 1 then the hyperplanes do not cover. Lemma 4.1: E_{k-1}[alpha_k^2] <= |S_k|^{-2} prod_{j<k} (1+3/((1-delta)|S_j|)). Distinctness enters only through non-parallelism (Lemma 4.3). P.
Use [LEAD]: Theorem 2.1 is already a statement about hyperplanes in a product S_1 x...xS_n, i.e. covers of (Z/p_1) x (Z/p_2) x ... The #203 torus Z^2/Lambda_S is NOT a product of hyperplane-type sets in the primes basis, but via CRT Z^2/Lambda_S -> product over primes of H_p; a coset of Lambda_p is the set of points whose p-component is a fixed element of H_p: this IS a 'hyperplane' x_p = c in prod_p H_p, when the CRT map Z^2 -> prod H_p is surjective (it is not: its image is a proper subgroup, of index gcd-type). Lemma 3.2's second-moment criterion could be re-run on the image subgroup (a sub-box). Uncovered density = measure of {x in image : x_p != c_p for all p}. This reformulation (a covering of a SUBGROUP of prod_p H_p by coordinate hyperplanes; coordinates are the primes) is the cleanest way to transport BBMST; the repo's DISTORTION.md appears to do equivalent work. Worth checking that the image-subgroup (not full product) is used.


### L7. [LEAD, strongest] Li-Wang-Yi: the distortion method as an abstract theorem on a finite set S with an inverse system
Huixi Li, Biao Wang, Shaoyun Yi, "On the minimum modulus problem in number fields", arXiv:2302.05946 (read via ar5iv, 2026-10-26; journal not on page).
Thm 2.1 (general framework, exact content as extracted): S finite, maps pi_j: S -> T_j (1<=j<=J), pi_0 = pi, an inverse system (pi_j(x)=pi_j(x') => pi_{j-1}(x)=pi_{j-1}(x')); sets B_j subset S; delta_j in [0,1/2]; fibre F_{j-1}(x); alpha_j(x)=|F_{j-1}(x) cap B_j|/|F_{j-1}(x)|; P_0 = a pi-measurable probability measure; P_j(x)=P_{j-1}(x) * [ 1_{x notin B_j}/(1-alpha_j(x)) if alpha_j(x)<delta_j ; (alpha_j(x) - 1_{x in B_j} delta_j)/(alpha_j(x)(1-delta_j)) if alpha_j(x)>=delta_j ]. With M_j^(1)=E_{j-1}[alpha_j], M_j^(2)=E_{j-1}[alpha_j^2]: if sum_j min{ M_j^(1), M_j^(2)/(4 delta_j(1-delta_j)) } < 1 then P_J(union B_j) < 1, i.e. the B_j do not cover S.
Thm 1.2: covering of O_K with multiplicity s and 'distinguishable' ideals (unique prime ideal factor of largest norm) has min_i ||I_i|| <= exp(c_K log^2(s+1)/loglog(s+2)). Lemma 3.3: (1) if delta_i=0 for i<j: M_j^(1) <= s * sum 1/||I|| over distinguishable I with ||I||>=||I_1||, P_min(I)=p_j; (2) M_j^(2) << s^2 (log||p_j||)^6/||p_j||^2. Proof: expand E_{j-1}[alpha_j^k] over tuples (r_l,H_l,i_l); multiplicity gives s^k choices of indices; CRT turns an intersection of classes into one class mod intersection of the H_l; Lemma 3.2: P_j(a+I) <= (1/||I||) prod_{p_i | I, i<=j} 1/(1-delta_i); for k=2 the product is <= 2^{omega(H_1 cap H_2)} and the remaining sum is an Euler product, giving (log ||p_j||)^6.
Use for #203 [LEAD]: take S = Z^2/Lambda_S (finite torus), P_0 uniform. Order the primes of the certificate by the LARGEST PRIME FACTOR q of e_p: j-th group = {p : P^+(e_p)=q_j}. Because H_p is cyclic, Z^2/Lambda_p = Z/e_p is cyclic, so each Lambda_p is exactly like a modulus e_p; the projections pi_j: Z^2/Lambda_S -> Z^2/(Lambda_{<j}) (lattice generated by the smaller groups) form an inverse system. Then Thm 2.1 gives non-covering as soon as sum_q min{M^(1)_q, M^(2)_q/(4 delta(1-delta))} < 1. The tail (all groups with q > y) is controlled by M^(2)_q << s^2 (log q)^6/q^2 UNCONDITIONALLY IN THE NUMBER OF PRIMES, which is exactly what is missing from a Sigma 1/e_p argument (that sum diverges over all primes, Sigma 1/p). What a prover must supply: the 2-D analogue of Lemma 3.3(2), i.e. E[alpha_j^2] = q^{-2} sum over pairs of lattices in group j of P(x in coset_1 cap coset_2) with the pair sum controlled. Here two lattices of the same index e' can have a LARGE intersection (index ~ e' instead of ~e'^2) iff they share a primitive vector, and then (Lemma below) they coincide; so the 'multiplicity' s should be defined as the max number of primes sharing one lattice, and a cross-term estimate for pairs of distinct lattices with Lambda_1 + Lambda_2 != Z^2 is needed (index of Lambda_1 cap Lambda_2 = e_1 e_2 / [Z^2 : Lambda_1+Lambda_2]). Sieving this out with Lemma 'short vectors' (Minkowski: Lambda_p has a nonzero vector of length <= (4e_p/3)^{1/2}... so p | 2^a 3^b - 1 with |a|,|b| << sqrt(e_p)) bounds the number of primes with e_p <= E by << E^{3/2}/log E unconditionally, and the number of pairs sharing a short vector. P for the quoted statements.
Elementary fact used above (proved here, not from a source): if v is primitive in Lambda (rank 2, Z^2/Lambda cyclic of order e) then Lambda = pi_v^{-1}(eZ) where pi_v: Z^2 -> Z^2/Zv = Z; so two lattices of equal index e containing the same primitive vector are equal. [P: standard.]

### L9. Elsholtz, "The distribution of sequences in residue classes", Proc. AMS 130 (2002), read via the author's page (math.tugraz.at/~elsholtz/WWW/papers/papers05procamsdistribution.pdf)
Theorem (paraphrase): if A subset [1,x], |A| >= c1 (log x)^r then for all but a c3-proportion of primes p <= y = c4 (log x)^{r+1}, A occupies >= c2 p^{r/(r+1)} residue classes (Gallagher larger sieve). A 'most p' statement; same verdict as L8. ### L11. Agent note: Pool size via lattice geometry [derived here; no external source]
For p >= 5 let Lambda_p = {(i,j): 2^i 3^j = 1 mod p}, index e_p, quotient cyclic. Minkowski's second theorem for the planar lattice gives nonzero (i,j) in Lambda_p with max(|i|,|j|) <= sqrt(e_p) (convex symmetric body of area 4e_p contains a nonzero lattice point). Hence p | 2^i 3^j - 1 for such (i,j), and #{p : e_p <= E} <= sum over (i,j) in box of omega(|2^i 3^j - 1|) << E * sqrt(E) (each number has O(sqrt(E)) prime factors >= 5). So #pool(E) << E^{3/2}, unconditional and effective. Granville-Pappalardi's Prop 2/3 describe the same lattices (triples (u,v,L)) with L = lcm(ord a, ord b).

### L14. [LEAD] What exactly is missing for an unconditional tail bound (derived here from L7; no external source for the last two steps)
Notation: c(e) = #{p>=5 : e_p = e}, pool(E) = #{p : e_p <= E}.
(a) [PROVED here] For cosets C, C' of lattices Lambda, Lambda' with cyclic quotients of orders H, H', P_unif(C cap C') <= 1/lcm(H,H') (index of Lambda cap Lambda' = HH'/[Z^2 : Lambda+Lambda'], and [Z^2:Lambda+Lambda'] divides gcd(H,H') since Z^2/(Lambda+Lambda') is a quotient of both cyclic groups). Hence every pair estimate in the 1-D distortion proofs (BBMST Lemma 4.1, KKL Lemma 3.2/3.3, LWY Lemma 3.3) holds in the torus Z^2/Lambda_S with the 1-D moduli replaced by e_p, with the same bound or better (the repo's DISTORTION.md (L2) already exploits the 'better': c^< = 1 unless forms align).
(b) Consequently the infinite tail (all groups with P^+(e_p) = q > y) costs at most sum_{q>y} C (log q)^6 q^{-2} * (multiplicity factor) with the multiplicity factor = max over (H,r) of c(H q^r)^2 in the diagonal/same-H terms, provided identical cosets are removed (minimal certificate: two primes with the same lattice and different cosets are disjoint events and contribute 0 to the cross term; with the same coset one prime is redundant).
(c) So the tail is controlled as soon as sup_{P^+(e)=q} c(e) <= q^{1/2-eps}, or on average sum_{e<=X} c(e)^2 << X^{1+eps}. What is known: pool(E) << E^{3/2}/log E (elementary: Minkowski gives a nonzero (i,j) in Lambda_p with |i|,|j| <= sqrt(e_p) and p | 2^i 3^j - 1; the same exponent 1+1/r appears as Lemma 8 of arXiv:1504.01550 [GRH-conditional upper bound for the number of p with |Gamma_p| <= t, t^{1+1/r}/log t]). So even GRH does not improve the exponent 3/2. Truth (data in HUB: 47583 primes with e_p in (4e5,8e5]) is pool(E) ~ 0.12 E. The gap E^{3/2} vs E is the missing arithmetic input; a proof must therefore avoid pool counts, OR use a Corvaja-Zannier / Hernandez-Luca type theorem to bound the number of primes common to two independent lattice vectors (below). 
(d) Corvaja-Zannier, Hernandez-Luca (as stated in A. Levin's slides, math.columbia.edu/~goldfeld/JNT-ALevin.pdf, and Corvaja's RIMS note 1710): for multiplicatively independent S-units u,v, log gcd(u-1,v-1) <= eps max(log|u|,log|v|) for all but finitely many pairs (exceptions in finitely many proper subtori). Applied to u=2^{a}3^{b}, v=2^{c}3^{d} with (a,b),(c,d) independent this says: for all but finitely many independent pairs, the primes p with BOTH vectors in Lambda_p have total log eps*max height, so the number of primes with Lambda_p containing two given independent vectors is <= eps*max|v_i| / log 5. All INEFFECTIVE (subspace theorem). BCZ (Math. Z. 243 (2003)): gcd(a^n-1,b^n-1) < exp(eps n); Adleman-Pomerance-Rumely lower bound: for infinitely many n, #{p : (p-1) | n} >= exp(C log n/loglog n) so gcd(2^n-1,3^n-1) can be large; Nguyen-Dang arXiv:2606.07959 shows gcd(a^n-1,b^n-1) satisfies no linear recurrence unless dependent, no effective n_0 given.

### L16. Result: the needed bound on c(e) is an OPEN CONJECTURE even for one base (Erdős)
Akbary-Yazdani, "On the greatest prime factor of some divisibility sequences", arXiv:1505.06500 (read via ar5iv and www.cs.uleth.ca/~akbary/Akbary-Yazdani.pdf). With E_a(r) = #{p : ord_p(a) = r}: Conjecture 1.2 (Erdős, formulated for a=2): E_a(r) << _eps r^eps for every eps>0. Felix-Murty: omega(a^n - 1) = sum_{d|n} E_a(d), so the conjecture gives omega(a^n-1) << n^eps. UNCONDITIONAL knowledge as stated there: omega(a^n-1) << n/log n (upper), and Prachar [Satz 2]: omega(a^n-1) >= exp(c log n/(loglog n)^2) for infinitely many n (lower). No unconditional bound on E_a(r) beyond the trivial one is stated; no average over n of omega(a^n-1) or E_a(r) is stated. Murty-Wong (ABC): P(a^n-1) >> n^{2-eps}; Stewart (unconditional): P(a^n-b^n) >= n^{1+1/(104 loglog n)}.
Meaning for #203: c(e) is the two-generator analogue of E_a(r) (primes with <2,3> of order exactly e; c(e) <= omega(gcd(2^e-1,3^e-1)) and sum_{d|n} c(d) = omega(gcd(2^n-1,3^n-1))). The input 'c(e) <= q^{1/2-eps}' needed in L14 is WEAKER than Erdős's conjecture (which would give c(e) << e^eps) but no unconditional bound of any power-saving type is known. Trivial bound: every p with e_p = e divides gcd(2^e-1,3^e-1) | 3^e - 1 and p >= e+1, so c(e) <= e log 3/log(e+1). BCZ makes this c(e) <= eps e/log e for e >= e_0(eps) (ineffective). Both are linear in e, i.e. useless for the tail. P (statements as quoted).

### L17. Counting primes with a small group <a,b> or <a>: the state of the art is the trivial divisor count

### L19. Direction-lemma dichotomy: what literature exists
- Counting facts used (elementary, derived here; not from a source): in AG(2,l) = F_l^2, l lines cover all points iff they form a full parallel class; l+1 lines cover all points when they are concurrent (all l+1 directions through one point: 1+(l+1)(l-1)=l^2). In the Z^2 certificate the fibre of the Sylow-l part is (Z/l^a)^2 and cosets with v_l(e_p)=1 are such affine lines, one direction per prime. The unit-direction condition of my direction lemma (L-section lead 3) fails at l exactly when the set of kernel directions at l is all of P^1(F_l).
- Jamison (1977) / Brouwer-Schrijver (1978) as stated in 'On blocking sets of affine spaces' (arXiv:math/9910084): a set meeting every hyperplane of AG(n,q) has >= n(q-1)+1 points; for n=2 any point set meeting all lines has >= 2q-1 points. Dual use: covers of AG(2,l) minus a point by lines avoiding it need >= 2(l-1) lines (Hegedus, arXiv:2405.16231, cites Jamison; his own Cor. 1.9: every point of F_l^2 needs more than 2l/e - 2 lines in an almost cover). Gives quantitative necessary conditions: if the l-fibre is covered by index-l cosets with few directions the count of lines needed is >= l (single direction) or about >= 2l-2 when one point is left to other primes. P for the quoted statements, the application is mine.
- Z.-W. Sun, 'Finite covers of groups by cosets or subgroups', arXiv:math/0501451 and 'On covers of abelian groups by cosets', math/0411144, are the only literature I found treating covers by cosets of arbitrary abelian groups (hence Z^2/Lambda); their results are about the SIZE k of minimal covers (Mycielski function), not about directions/unit lines. Berger-Felzenbaum-Fraenkel's 'lattice-geometric method' is mentioned by Guo-Sun (math/0412217) for necessary conditions on disjoint coverings, text not opened. Cochrane-Myerson / Jin-Myerson (homogeneous Z^2) not accessible. I found NO paper stating the direction dichotomy; it must be proved from scratch (it is elementary: for each prime l, avoid the finite set of kernel directions K_l subset P^1(F_l) by CRT over l | lcm e_p; a unit vector exists iff K_l != P^1(F_l) for all l).
